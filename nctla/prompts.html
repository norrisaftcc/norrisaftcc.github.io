NCTLA · 23 October 2026 · Generated Is Not Learned

Five prompts that keep the thinking with the learner

Andrew Norris, Fayetteville Technical Community College. Each prompt gives an AI tool one job and leaves the reasoning to the student. Copy one, replace the words in [brackets], and run it with the student beside you.

What did the student actually have to think about?

Start with the first line. Each prompt opens with who the student is and what the tool must stay inside: “I am a first-year English student in ENG 111. Stay within my own claim about the American Dream.” Without it, the tool guesses.

Use it with a tutor. Every prompt works better with a person watching the student work. Tutors already lead a student to an answer without giving it.

Check what comes back. The tool can be wrong, and it sounds right either way. Never trust, always verify.

End with transfer. Close the AI, give a similar problem, and see what the student can still do.

Role 1 of 5 · Coach who steps back

Guided learning, with the help fading

Use when
The learner is new to the topic.
Principle
Worked example, then fading: show one, do one with blanks, do one alone.
Why it works
It explains first, then pulls back, then asks for reasoning.
# Teach me [topic] step by step
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- I am new to [topic]. Use plain words. Define each new term one time.
- Show one worked example first. Explain each step.
- Then give me a similar problem with some steps blank. Wait for my answer.
- Then give me one problem to do alone. Do not help until I ask.
- When I am stuck, give one hint. Do not give the answer.
- Ask me to explain my reasoning before you tell me if I am correct.
Filled in: weight-based dosage calculation
# Teach me weight-based dosage calculation (mg/kg, with a pounds-to-kilograms step) step by step
- I am a first-semester nursing student in Nursing Fundamentals. Stay within oral doses calculated by body weight; no IV drip rates yet.
- I am new to weight-based dosage calculation (mg/kg, with a pounds-to-kilograms step). Use plain words. Define each new term one time.
- Show one worked example first. Explain each step.
- Then give me a similar problem with some steps blank. Wait for my answer.
- Then give me one problem to do alone. Do not help until I ask.
- When I am stuck, give one hint. Do not give the answer.
- Ask me to explain my reasoning before you tell me if I am correct.
Filled in: z-scores, MAT 152
# Teach me z-scores in introductory statistics step by step
- I am a first-year student in MAT 152, Statistical Methods. Stay within z-scores for one data set; no hypothesis tests yet.
- I am new to z-scores in introductory statistics. Use plain words. Define each new term one time.
- Show one worked example first. Explain each step.
- Then give me a similar problem with some steps blank. Wait for my answer.
- Then give me one problem to do alone. Do not help until I ask.
- When I am stuck, give one hint. Do not give the answer.
- Ask me to explain my reasoning before you tell me if I am correct.

Good

  • It explains before it asks, so no Socratic trap.
  • It asks for the fading by name.
  • It asks for reasoning before it says right or wrong.

Watch for

  • After a few turns, the tool can drift back to telling.
  • The student can still type “just give me the answer.”
  • The worked example can be wrong. Check it.

Role 2 of 5 · Question generator

Vocabulary map

Use when
The learner does not know what to ask.
Principle
Activate what they know. Name what they do not know.
Why it works
It turns unknown unknowns into a list they bring to you.
# List the terms I do not know yet about [topic]
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- I am a beginner in [topic].
- List 10 terms that an expert uses and a beginner may not know.
- Give each term a one-sentence definition.
- Mark the 3 terms to learn first.
Filled in: first-year nursing
# List the terms I do not know yet about patient assessment
- I am a first-semester nursing student in Nursing Fundamentals. Stay within terms used in a basic head-to-toe assessment.
- I am a beginner in patient assessment.
- List 10 terms that an expert uses and a beginner may not know.
- Give each term a one-sentence definition.
- Mark the 3 terms to learn first.
Filled in: literary analysis, ENG 111
# List the terms I do not know yet about literary analysis of The Great Gatsby
- I am a first-year English student in ENG 111. Stay within terms for writing a literary analysis essay.
- I am a beginner in literary analysis of The Great Gatsby.
- List 10 terms that an expert uses and a beginner may not know.
- Give each term a one-sentence definition.
- Mark the 3 terms to learn first.

Good

  • Fast and low risk.
  • It gives the session an agenda.
  • It helps the student who does not know what to ask.

Watch for

  • A definition can be wrong or too technical.
  • A list is not understanding. It can become one more thing to reread.
  • Ten terms can swamp a beginner.

Role 3 of 5 · Contrast generator

Two diagrams, explained back

Use when
The idea has parts, steps, or flow.
Principle
Contrasting cases: the learner finds the difference and explains it.
Why it works
The tool draws. The learner compares and explains.
# Draw [process or idea] as a diagram
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- Show each part or step as a labeled box.
- Use arrows to show order or cause.
- Then draw a second version with one step wrong.
- Do not explain either diagram.
- Ask me which version is right, and why. Then tell me which parts of my explanation are wrong.
Filled in: ER triage, nursing
# Draw the normal ER triage process as a diagram
- I am a first-semester nursing student in Nursing Fundamentals. Stay within the steps from arrival to the triage level; not treatment.
- Show each part or step as a labeled box.
- Use arrows to show order or cause.
- Then draw a second version with one step wrong.
- Do not explain either diagram.
- Ask me which version is right, and why. Then tell me which parts of my explanation are wrong.
Filled in: blood flow through the heart
# Draw the path of blood through the heart as a diagram
- I am a first-year student in BIO 168, Anatomy and Physiology I. Stay within the four chambers and four valves; not the electrical system.
- Show each part or step as a labeled box.
- Use arrows to show order or cause.
- Then draw a second version with one step wrong.
- Do not explain either diagram.
- Ask me which version is right, and why. Then tell me which parts of my explanation are wrong.

Good

  • Comparing is where the thinking happens.
  • The student explains. The tool does not.
  • It works for any process or flow.

Watch for

  • Some tools draw badly. Text diagrams get messy.
  • The “right” diagram can be wrong too.
  • Check both before the student sees them.

Role 4 of 5 · Error analyst

Find the error

Use when
The learner can almost do it.
Principle
Learning from errors: find it, show why, repair it.
Why it works
AI output becomes a candidate to argue with, not an answer key.
# Write a short solution to [problem] that has one error
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- Use an error that beginners often make.
- Do not tell me where the error is.
- When I find it, ask me to show why it is an error.
- When I cannot find it, give one hint at a time.

Or, to check the learner’s own work:

# Find the first place I went off track
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- Here is my work on [problem].
- Find the first step where my reasoning goes wrong.
- Do not fix it. Ask me one question that helps me find it.

- Use an error that beginners often make.

- Here is my work on a weight-based dosage problem (mg/kg, with a pounds-to-kilograms step).
- Find the first step where my reasoning goes wrong.
- Do not fix it. Ask me one question that helps me find it.

# Find the first place I went off track

- Here is my work on a z-score problem for one test result.
- Find the first step where my reasoning goes wrong.
- Do not fix it. Ask me one question that helps me find it.

Good

  • The output becomes a claim to test.
  • It targets the errors beginners make.
  • Steps 1 to 4 of the session rule, in one prompt.

Watch for

  • The tool may plant two errors, or none.
  • A weak student can learn the error. End by stating the right method.
  • Dosage: label the work as practice, never as a reference.

Role 5 of 5 · Practice simulator

Teach it back

Use when
The learner thinks they have it.
Principle
Self-explanation: only the learner’s own words count.
Why it works
Their words stay theirs. The tool only checks and asks.
# Check my explanation of [topic]
- I am a [level] student in [course]. Stay within [scope].
- I will explain [topic] in my own words.
- Do not rewrite my explanation.
- Tell me which parts are correct and which parts are not.
- Ask me one question about the weakest part.
Filled in: heart valves, BIO 168
# Check my explanation of how the heart valves keep blood moving one way
- I am a first-year student in BIO 168, Anatomy and Physiology I. Stay within the four heart valves and what each one does.
- I will explain how the heart valves keep blood moving one way in my own words.
- Do not rewrite my explanation.
- Tell me which parts are correct and which parts are not.
- Ask me one question about the weakest part.
Filled in: the American Dream in Gatsby, ENG 111
# Check my explanation of how The Great Gatsby treats the American Dream
- I am a first-year English student in ENG 111. Stay within my own claim about the American Dream; no outside sources.
- I will explain how The Great Gatsby treats the American Dream in my own words.
- Do not rewrite my explanation.
- Tell me which parts are correct and which parts are not.
- Ask me one question about the weakest part.

Good

  • The student does the explaining.
  • It shows gaps the student cannot see.
  • It works between sessions.

Watch for

  • The tool may rewrite the explanation anyway.
  • It can praise a weak answer. Tell it to be strict.
  • The student must type a real explanation. That takes effort.

One more · End of session

The transfer check

Use it at the end of a session. Then close the AI and let the student try.

After helping with this problem, create one new problem that uses the same underlying skill but has different surface details. Do not provide the solution until the student attempts it.

The pattern behind all five

ASK → ATTEMPT → HINT → RETRY → EXPLAIN → TRANSFER

ASK → GENERATE → COPY → SUBMIT

Generated is not learned. Whoever writes the reasoning keeps it.
